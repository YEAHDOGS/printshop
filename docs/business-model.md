# DOGS Print Shop — business model (2026-09-09)

Two lanes, picked in order. Brandon is cash-tight, so the opening move must
cost ~$0.

## Lane A — digital STL file sales (START HERE)

- What: parametric, well-documented 3D models sold as downloads
  (this repo's `assets/*.scad` + exported STLs).
- Price points: $5–15 per model (Etsy/Cults precedent for functional prints).
- Margin: ~100% minus marketplace fees. No printer, no filament, no
  shipping, no inventory, no customer "my print failed" support beyond the
  file itself.
- Channels: Etsy, Cults3D, Printables (free teaser models drive traffic to
  paid parametric ones).
- First product: parametric car seat gap filler
  (`docs/research/seat-gap-filler.md` for why the STL beats the physical).

## Lane B — printed-on-demand physicals (LATER)

- What: print and ship physical products per order.
- Requires: a 3D printer in-house (see open question below) OR a
  fulfillment partner (Craftcloud / Shapeways / Xometry-style services take
  the print-and-ship job for a cut).
- Only makes sense for products retail can't clone in foam — organizer
  gadgets, custom-fit parts, things with moving features. Never for the
  plain wedge (unit economics lose to $10 Amazon foam; see research doc).
- Price points: $25–40+ premium for the gadget version.

## Marketing: organic first, ads later

Brandon mentioned running ads. Recommendation: **no paid ad spend until one
product proves itself.** The proof is cheap and he already owns the format:

1. Short-form video of the print + the pain point ("watch my keys stop
   falling into the seat crack") — his own car, his own story.
2. Free teaser STL on Printables drives traffic to the paid parametric
   version.
3. Paid ads only after a product has organic conversion data to scale.
